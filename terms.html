MacCleaner removes files you choose. Please read this page before you delete anything with it.

Effective date: 22 October 2026 · Applies to MacCleaner 1.0 and later.

In short

You decide what is removed, and you use MacCleaner at your own risk

  • MacCleaner suggests files that are often safe to remove. It cannot know which files matter to you, and its suggestions can be wrong.
  • You review and confirm every removal. Keep a current backup (for example with Time Machine) before you clean.
  • Removed items go to the Trash, or to the optional quarantine folder. Once the Trash is emptied or the quarantine period ends, they are gone.
  • The app is provided "as is", under Apple's standard licence terms described below.

2. Your responsibility when removing files

  • You choose. MacCleaner never removes anything on its own. Every removal happens only after you select items, review them, run a dry run and confirm.
  • Labels are guidance, not a guarantee. The Safe, Caution, Risky and Dangerous labels and Smart Clean's pre-selection come from rules about where apps and developer tools usually keep regenerable data. Other apps can change where and how they store data at any time, so a label can be wrong for your Mac.
  • Removing caches has side effects. Apps and developer tools may need to download, rebuild or re-index data afterwards, which can take time and network traffic. Removing virtual environments, toolchains, simulators, archives or AI models can make projects or apps stop working until you reinstall them.
  • Uninstalling removes app data. Leftover files can contain an app's settings, documents or account data. Quit the app first and check the list before you confirm.
  • Duplicates and similar photos. Two files with the same content can still serve different purposes (for example, a copy inside a project or a synced folder). Similar photos are only visually close and are never selected for you.
  • Back up first. Keep a current backup of anything you can't afford to lose.

3. Safeguards and their limits

MacCleaner moves items to the Trash or the quarantine folder instead of erasing them, runs a dry run before every removal, refuses to touch protected system and personal locations, and keeps a local log of what it removed. These safeguards reduce mistakes but cannot rule them out. They do not protect items after you empty the Trash, after the quarantine period ends, or files that macOS or another app changes in the meantime.

Space figures are estimates based on what the app can see in the folders you granted. The space macOS reports as freed can differ, for example because of local snapshots, cloned files or purgeable storage.
