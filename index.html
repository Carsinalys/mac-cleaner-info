Getting started

MacCleaner is designed to work in under a minute — here's the quick start:

  1. Grant folder access. On first launch, select your home folder when asked. This is a macOS security requirement — MacCleaner only scans folders you explicitly grant access to.
  2. Optionally grant /Applications access. If you want to uninstall apps completely (including their leftover files), select your /Applications folder next. You can skip this and return later from Settings.
  3. Run a scan. Click "Start Scan" on the Overview tab. The scan finds developer caches, app leftovers, duplicates, and large files. Results appear in under a minute on most Macs.
  4. Explore what was found. Each category in the results shows a file tree — click any file for Quick Look preview, or right-click to reveal in Finder or exclude it from future scans.
  5. Clean safely. Smart Clean pre-selects only safe-to-delete items, or pick manually. Either way, every deletion goes through Trash — nothing is permanently lost without your explicit confirmation.

Troubleshooting

MacCleaner cannot see some of my files

macOS restricts apps to folders you've explicitly granted access to. If MacCleaner seems to miss files that you know exist (e.g., in ~/Library or other user accounts), the folder containing them hasn't been granted. You can:

  • Grant the parent folder (e.g., your entire home folder) instead of a subfolder.
  • Check Settings → Scan Exclusions — maybe you excluded that path earlier.
  • Note that system files, other users' accounts, and macOS "System Data" are never visible to any Mac App Store app.

The disk map total is smaller than what Finder shows

This is expected. The map only covers folders you granted access to; Finder shows everything including system data, other users, and protected folders that no third-party app can see. MacCleaner's numbers for the folders it covers are more accurate: it reports actual on-disk allocation (not logical file size) and handles sparse files like Docker.raw correctly.

Scanning is very slow on my external drive

If the drive is a network volume (SMB, NFS) mounted on your Mac, a slow response is the network. MacCleaner has a 60-second timeout per subtree — if the drive hangs, the affected files are skipped, and the scan continues. Your results may be incomplete in that case.

Smart Clean or individual deletion says "Permission denied"

Files locked by another app or with restrictive permissions may refuse to move to Trash. Try closing the app that owns those files, then scan again. If the problem persists, remove the folder from Scan Exclusions and re-grant access.

The sunburst visualizer shows a smaller total than the scan

Both numbers are correct for their scope. The Overview scan reports the total of reclaimable (deletable) items, while the visualizer maps the entire home folder — including files that aren't flagged for deletion. The sunburst is a breakdown of your full disk usage, not just the "junk".

Frequently asked questions

Is MacCleaner safe to use?

Yes — that's the core design. Every deletion goes through macOS's Trash (or an optional quarantine folder), protected system paths like /System and /Library/Apple are locked before any scan starts, and a dry-run pass shows you exactly what will be deleted before anything happens. Nothing is permanently erased without your explicit confirmation.

Does MacCleaner send any data anywhere?

No. All scanning and cleanup is local. There are no analytics SDKs, no crash-reporting tools, no advertising. The only network traffic is Apple's own StoreKit purchase verification — which is handled by macOS and required for App Store purchases. See the privacy policy.

Why does Finder show more "used" space than MacCleaner?

MacCleaner only scans folders you've granted it access to. macOS "System Data", other users' accounts, and protected system volumes are invisible to every Mac App Store app. This is a privacy feature, not a limitation of MacCleaner specifically — no cleaner can see those areas.

Does MacCleaner delete duplicates of hard-linked files?

MacCleaner counts hard-linked files only once per scan (counting each hard link separately would inflate the reported reclaimable space). If the same physical file appears at multiple paths as hard links, the first occurrence is counted and subsequent ones show as 0 bytes.

Is MacCleaner a subscription?

No. You download the app free, explore everything it finds, and unlock the full experience with a one-time $9.99 Lifetime License. No subscription, no recurring charges, ever.

How do I request a refund?

Apple handles all App Store billing and refunds. Request one at reportaproblem.apple.com using the Apple ID that made the purchase. If something in the app caused the problem, email us too — we'd rather fix it.

Which macOS versions are supported?

macOS 14 Sonoma and later, on both Apple Silicon and Intel Macs.

Can I use MacCleaner on external drives?

Yes. In the Visualizer tab, click the plus button and select any mounted volume or folder. You can also drag a folder onto the MacCleaner icon in Finder. Scanning external drives works the same as scanning your home folder, and you can clean files from them the same way.

Can I exclude folders from scans?

Yes. Right-click any folder in a scan result and choose "Exclude from Scans," or manage exclusions in Settings. Excluded paths are skipped entirely; remove them any time to include them again.

Privacy in one paragraph

All processing is local — your data never leaves your Mac

MacCleaner has no account system, no analytics SDKs, no advertising, and no third-party code. It does not track you. The only data it stores is your own settings and scan results, kept locally on your Mac and removed with the app.
